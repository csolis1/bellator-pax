Inside The Fighter’s Mind: Bongo the Fight Pirate

By C.I Solis • May 28, 2026

At the age when most fighters are still figuring out how to breathe between rounds, Bongo has already carved his way through kickboxing, Muay Thai, boxing, Karate, and Taekwondo. He looks at the belts on the table like old friends — not as symbols of dominance, but as memories weighted with gravitas. He can tell you where he was, who his opponent was, what was at stake, what hurt, what almost broke. There is no bragging in his stories. Only fond remembrance. But what makes him most sentimental is his flag. The black pirate flag bearing a skull fused with the Orthodox symbols that give him purpose.

It is his sacred tradition: for every title won, he leaves the flag behind at the gym where it happened. A marker. A thank-you. A quiet detonation made beautiful through sweat, blood and purpose. And now, Striker Fight Center is home to his latest.

If you ever have the pleasure — or cosmic misfortune — of encountering Bongo, you will understand three things immediately.

Number 1: Bongo is not built like you or me.

He is unpredictably fast and unnervingly accurate. His teeps and side kicks will send you flying regardless of height or weight. He knows exactly where to hit you and exactly how hard.

His limbs seem engineered from steel tension cables, the kind used to hold up suspension bridges. His sweeps will fold your body into itself like a poorly assembled IKEA shelf.

He possesses an endless reserve of energy capable of oscillating between chaos and calm, explosive and deliberate. The basic human instinct of fear appears to have been burned out of him completely by his faith.

As a devout Eastern Orthodox Christian, he operates like a man who has signed over the deed to both body and soul.

As a devout Eastern Orthodox Christian, he operates like a man who has signed over the deed to both body and soul.

That kind of conviction elevates discipline into something else entirely.

Number 3: You will never meet another Bongo

There is only one of him, and the universe still appears to be trying to understand how it allowed that to happen.

Marine veteran. Thirty-two title belts across kickboxing, boxing, and Muay Thai, with more accolades in Taekwondo and Karate — and it won’t stop there. But beyond being an extraordinarily accomplished and decorated fighter, he is, without question, one of the most unique human beings I have ever met.

But beyond being an extraordinarily accomplished and decorated fighter, he is, without question, one of the most unique human beings I have ever met.

Outside the ring, he becomes a kind of chimera.

He will make you laugh with strange noises, bizarre impressions, and sudden yet weirdly graceful dances that defy explanation. You will not understand why you are laughing. And frankly, it doesn’t matter.

In the next breath, he will sit down and explain the history of Orthodoxy with the depth of a theologian. He unpacks it all — Councils, schisms, liturgy, icons, with the same passion he brings to fighting, but with a calm intelligence that feels wildly disproportionate to his age.

He is absurdly competent at nearly everything involving rhythm, dance, combat, or violence, often mastering new disciplines with an unsettling lack of resistance, occasionally winning competitions after only a few weeks of learning.

And when you think there is nothing left for Bongo to prove, he is already somewhere else — collecting another belt, another story, another room to infect with his beautifully insane contagion of motion and meaning.

It is no surprise that Bongo has been given the opportunity to move east to Thailand. Of course the deep, ancient fight culture of the East called him over. Places like that do not simply invite people. They pull in anomalies and summon phenomena.

And Bongo is, without question, a phenomenon.
