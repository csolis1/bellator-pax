Design is the silence
between intention and form.
Korea banned martial arts under Japanese occupation. People kept training anyway, behind closed doors and in rice paddies. Taekwondo was built out of that refusal.
Practiced in Secret
For 35 years under Japanese rule, Korean martial arts were pushed underground. Instructors kept teaching anyway, quietly, in back rooms and border towns, folding in what they remembered of taekkyon and subak alongside what they'd picked up from Okinawan karate.
Nine Schools, One Name
After liberation in 1945, those separate lineages surfaced as nine competing kwans, each with its own curriculum and its own idea of what came next. General Choi Hong Hi pushed to unify them under a single name in 1955: taekwondo — the way of the foot and fist.
From Local to Olympic
The sport split into two federations, ITF and WT, each shaping the rules differently. WT's version kept pushing toward the international stage — a demonstration sport in 1988 and 2000, then a full medal event, turning a wartime discipline into a global one.
